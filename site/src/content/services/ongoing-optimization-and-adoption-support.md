## Make implementation useful beyond launch

Deploying an AI tool does not, by itself, establish that it has become a useful part of the organization. Teams still need to incorporate the solution into everyday responsibilities, understand how the workflow performs, and identify when a refinement is needed.

No Black Swan's Ongoing Optimization and Adoption Support service is designed for the period after implementation. It focuses on helping teams operationalize deployed solutions, evaluate their performance, and refine them as needed.

The purpose is to support the transition from a configured solution to a workflow that the business can use in practice. The precise support required depends on the implementation, the team's needs, and the scope agreed for the engagement.

## Bring the solution into day-to-day operations

A deployed solution exists within a wider business process. Employees may need to understand where it fits, how it relates to existing work, and what responsibilities remain with people. Systems and workflows also need to be considered as a whole, particularly when an AI tool has been connected to existing processes.

Adoption support focuses on the practical task of embedding the agreed solution in everyday operations. It is not the same as assuming that a tool will be used effectively simply because configuration and testing are complete.

The appropriate activities depend on the workflow and the agreed engagement. The intended direction is clear: bring the solution into the organization's work and consider whether the operating process is functioning as intended.

## Evaluate performance against an agreed purpose

Evaluation should begin with an understanding of what the solution was meant to improve. The business and implementation scope should establish appropriate success measures before work begins. Those measures can then provide a basis for reviewing the deployed solution.

The evaluation should distinguish intended outcomes from observed results. A project may have an operational objective, but a claim that the objective has been achieved needs evidence. No Black Swan does not publish unsupported performance percentages or quantified client savings.

The relevant evaluation method depends on the workflow, the intended outcome, and the information available. The engagement should clarify what will be reviewed and how observations will inform decisions about continued use or refinement.

## Identify and make appropriate refinements

Business workflows and their operating requirements may reveal issues that were not evident during initial planning. Performance observations may also identify places where a deployed solution needs to be adjusted. Ongoing Optimization and Adoption Support provides a service context for reviewing those findings and refining the solution as appropriate.

Refinement should remain tied to the business need and the agreed scope. Not every observed issue necessarily requires a change to the underlying tool. The appropriate response may involve clarifying a process, addressing an operational dependency, revisiting a configuration, or determining that further implementation work is needed.

The precise work depends on the solution and the problem identified. No particular monitoring product, service-level commitment, response time, or guaranteed frequency of optimization has been specified in the company information. Those details should be agreed rather than inferred from the service name.

## Connect adoption to operational ownership

AI initiatives can become fragmented when the responsibility for day-to-day operation is unclear. The broader No Black Swan approach is to connect systems, people, decisions, and workflows around an outcome.

Support after implementation should therefore consider how the deployed solution fits within the organization's actual operating practices. A tool used by one person in isolation may not achieve the intended wider benefit unless the relevant process, handoffs, and responsibilities are understood.

The goal is not simply to keep a tool available. It is to help the organization use the solution as part of its work, evaluate the performance that matters, and identify sensible improvements.

## How support is scoped

The engagement begins with a discussion of the solution already deployed, its intended purpose, and the adoption or performance questions that need attention. The parties can establish the workflow under review, the current constraints, relevant success measures, and the work needed to support continued use.

Scope should clarify the responsibilities, activities, evaluation approach, and commercial terms. It should also establish whether the work concerns adoption, performance evaluation, refinements, or a combination of these activities.

Ongoing support is a distinct service. It should not be assumed that every implementation engagement includes unlimited optimization or indefinite assistance after launch. The duration, cadence, response expectations, and boundaries of support need to be agreed for the particular engagement.

## When this service is relevant

Ongoing Optimization and Adoption Support may be useful when an AI solution has already been deployed and the organization needs help incorporating it into daily operations. It may also suit a business that wants to evaluate a solution against its intended objectives and identify whether refinement is warranted.

If no solution has been implemented yet, the appropriate next step may be assessment, strategy, integration, or automation work. If the implementation already exists, the discovery conversation can focus on its current operation and the outcomes the business is trying to achieve.
