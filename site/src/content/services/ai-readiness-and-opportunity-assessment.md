## Understand where AI can help before investing in tools

An AI project should start with a clear understanding of the business problem. Without that starting point, teams can invest in tools before knowing which workflows matter, whether a proposed use case is feasible, or how the work fits into existing operations.

No Black Swan's AI Readiness and Opportunity Assessment provides a structured review of the organization's current state. The purpose is to identify where AI may deliver the most value relative to effort, feasibility, and risk. The work is designed for businesses that want a practical basis for deciding what to investigate, prioritize, or implement next.

The assessment is relevant when leadership wants a practical AI plan, teams are spending too much time on manual work, or previous experiments have not translated into operational improvements.

## What the assessment examines

### Business goals and operational friction

The assessment starts with the business objectives behind the AI question. It considers what the organization wants to improve, how current workflows operate, and where manual or repetitive steps create friction. The point is to identify opportunities in the context of the work the business needs to do.

A useful review looks beyond an isolated task. A workflow can involve information moving between people, decisions being made at different stages, and systems that need to work together. Understanding those relationships helps reveal whether an apparent opportunity is practical and what other conditions may influence it.

### Current AI use and readiness

Organizations may already have employees experimenting with AI tools without a shared approach across the business. An assessment considers current AI use alongside existing workflows and constraints. The aim is to understand what has been tried, which operational problems remain, and whether separate activities point toward a larger opportunity.

Readiness also depends on the context of the proposed work. Relevant questions may concern feasibility, effort, risk, and the practical requirements for putting a solution into use. The appropriate review should be grounded in the specific workflow and the organization's circumstances rather than assume that every AI use case can be deployed in the same way.

### Opportunities, value, and risk

Potential use cases need to be compared, not simply collected. No Black Swan considers opportunities against value, feasibility, risk, and effort. These factors provide a way to distinguish a potentially useful improvement from an idea that requires too much work, presents unresolved risks, or does not address an important business need.

The resulting view is intended to help the organization determine where to focus attention. An assessment does not mean that every opportunity should proceed. Some may merit further investigation, some may be suitable for implementation planning, and others may not justify investment under the current circumstances.

## What happens during the engagement

The work begins with a discovery conversation about your business, existing AI use, operational friction, and desired improvements. That discussion establishes the questions that the assessment needs to address.

The next step is to understand the relevant business goals, workflows, and constraints. Potential opportunities can then be considered in relation to value, feasibility, risk, and effort. Once the findings have been reviewed, they can provide a basis for deciding whether to pursue strategy and advisory work, hands-on implementation, automation, or another appropriate next step.

The actual scope depends on the needs of the business. The assessment should be defined clearly enough that its focus, boundaries, and expected output are understood before work begins.

## What the assessment helps you decide

The purpose is to create a better-informed basis for action. Depending on the findings and agreed scope, the business may be better positioned to:

- Identify workflows that merit further AI investigation.
- Compare possible opportunities using consistent business criteria.
- Understand constraints that could affect feasibility or implementation effort.
- Separate useful, practical opportunities from experiments without a clear operational purpose.
- Decide whether further assessment, a strategy roadmap, or implementation planning is the appropriate next step.

These are decision benefits, not a promise of a specific financial or productivity result. No Black Swan does not publish quantified savings or guaranteed outcomes.

## When this service is a good starting point

An assessment may be appropriate if your organization knows it wants to make better use of AI but has not decided where to begin. It can also help if current experiments are scattered across teams, if repetitive work remains unresolved, or if leadership needs to understand the opportunity before committing resources to implementation.

If you already have a clear, sufficiently assessed use case, a more direct strategy or implementation discussion may be appropriate. The discovery conversation helps clarify how much investigation is still needed.
