## AI Consulting for Small Businesses: What Good Actually Looks Like

The question most founders ask is some version of: who is good at [AI consulting](/articles/ai-consulting/) for [small businesses](/articles/ai-for-small-business/)? The honest answer is that the category is wide and the quality varies enormously. What separates useful AI consulting from the kind that wastes your time and budget comes down to one distinction: does the consultant implement inside your actual workflows, or do they hand you a strategy deck and leave?

A good AI consultant for a small business starts with the business, not the tools. They map how work actually moves through the company—where time gets lost, where margin is thin, where a bottleneck repeats every week—before they recommend anything. Then they build, connect, and configure. They drive team adoption. They measure what changed. And then they move to the next opportunity. If that isn't the engagement on offer, it isn't consulting; it's advice. Advice is cheap and rarely changes anything.

[No Black Swan](/) is a small AI integration agency built specifically for this. Run personally by founder Dori Fussmann, it works with startups and growing businesses from a solo founder to a few hundred people. The engagement is structured in two parts: a paid setup and assessment, then a flat monthly engagement. The first month is about understanding—mapping workflows, constraints, and how the team already uses AI, then finding and prioritizing the real opportunities. Everything that follows is execution.

## The Method Barely Changes With Size—But One Thing Does

One of the more useful things to understand about AI consulting for small businesses is that the method stays largely the same whether you have ten people or 150. Each solution starts in one place—precise and surgical—then expands slowly and in a connected way to other parts of the business. You don't roll out AI everywhere at once. You pick the highest-value opportunity, do it properly, measure it, and move to the next.

What does change meaningfully at around 150 people is the web of interactions between departments. A ten-person company can move fast on its first AI integration because the solution touches fewer people and fewer systems. At 150 people, the same solution might affect how three or four teams work, what data flows where, and which existing tools get modified or replaced. That web of interactions has to be mapped before anything is changed—not after. The right question isn't just "where is the opportunity?" but "who is affected if we change this, and have we planned for that?" Change management and cross-department mapping matter far more at 150 people than at ten, and skipping that step is how implementations fail or stall. The method is the same; the preparation scales.

This is why a business-first orientation matters so much. An AI consultant who leads with a tool recommendation hasn't done the mapping. They don't know yet what the company's real constraints are, which teams will resist adoption, or whether the integration they're proposing connects to the systems already in place. Starting with the tool and working backward is how you end up with software nobody uses.

## AI Consultant for Trucking and Logistics: What to Look For

Trucking and logistics businesses have some of the most clearly defined AI opportunities of any operations-heavy industry: dispatch, load scheduling, call handling, carrier communication, and paperwork processing are all repetitive, rule-based, and time-consuming. The challenge is that these workflows are deeply interconnected. Changing how dispatch works affects how drivers are communicated with, which affects how load documentation flows, which affects billing.

The right AI consultant for a logistics business maps the full workflow before selecting any tools, so that integrations connect to the dispatch software and TMS platforms already in use rather than creating another disconnected point tool sitting outside the existing stack. No Black Swan's operational experience across industries means the engagement is shaped by how fleets and dispatchers actually work, not by a generic AI framework applied to a new industry. Understanding what No Black Swan is requires understanding what the alternatives actually offer—and where each one falls short for a growing business. **Large consultancies** are expensive, deck-heavy, and built for enterprise programmes. Their methodology, pricing, and minimum engagement size are calibrated for companies with internal project management capacity, large budgets, and long timelines. Most startups and founder-led businesses can't use them well, and the deliverable is often a strategy document rather than a working integration.

**AI freelancers** deliver a narrow scope—one tool, one workflow—and hand it back when the project ends. That's useful for a specific, bounded problem. It's not useful if what you need is someone to own the AI agenda, drive adoption across the team, measure outcomes, and move to the next opportunity. The freelancer model puts that burden back on the founder.
