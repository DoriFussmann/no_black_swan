## What a Forward Deployed Engineer Is, in Plain Terms

The term "forward deployed" comes from the military. It means stationed at the front—where the actual work happens—rather than at headquarters issuing instructions from a distance. That origin is a useful guide to what a forward deployed engineer (FDE) does: this is a technical person who works inside a client's business to build, integrate, and ship AI or software into that company's real operations. They don't send recommendations from an office. They show up where the dispatchers, estimators, and operations managers actually work, figure out what's broken or slow, and fix it with AI.

The role was popularized by Palantir Technologies, which used this title for staff by 2009. For years it remained a Palantir-specific oddity. That changed quickly: large companies including Anthropic, OpenAI, Amazon Web Services, Microsoft, and Google Cloud announced new divisions oriented around forward-deployed engineering in 2026.

LinkedIn's January 2026 labor-market report found that forward deployed engineering roles grew 42-fold between 2023 and 2025, compared with 13-fold growth for AI engineer roles. The gap between those two numbers says something important: building AI is easier than making AI work inside a real business.

In the market today, the FDE title has come to mean something broader than it did at Palantir. It now describes an outside owner who knows AI and knows how to run it—someone who is both an AI domain expert and an operations domain expert, embedded inside the business rather than advising from the outside. That dual expertise is the whole point. An engineer who understands only the technology will configure a tool that nobody uses. An operator who understands only the business will spot the right problem but lack the hands to solve it. The FDE role sits at the intersection.

For companies that are not technology businesses, a cleaner plain-English name for this function is **embedded AI operator**: someone who joins your operations from outside and makes AI work in them. The job is not to build AI models or write research papers. It is to take the AI that already exists, connect it to the workflows your team actually uses, and own the result until it is genuinely running. FDE and embedded AI operator describe the same function; embedded AI operator just travels better in non-tech environments, which is where the need is often greatest.

## What a Forward Deployed Engineer Does Day to Day

The work follows a repeating loop. Before any tool is touched, the embedded AI operator talks to the people doing the actual work—the ops manager routing trucks, the estimator pricing jobs, the account manager fielding inbound calls—to understand where friction, cost, and lost time are actually sitting. Discovery is the most underestimated phase of this work, and skipping it is the fastest way to build something nobody uses.

Once the high-value opportunities are mapped and prioritized, the engineer configures, connects, and sometimes builds the AI layer that fits into the workflows those people already follow. In practice, forward-deployed engineers help companies integrate AI with internal databases, APIs, and operational software, ensuring the technology can operate within existing infrastructure. That means the new AI tool does not sit in a separate tab that people have to remember to open—it surfaces in the system the team already relies on, at the moment they need it.

After the build comes adoption. The embedded engineer acts as a change agent within the customer organization, redesigning workflows and job functions for effective AI integration, guiding adoption of new processes, training teams, and helping integrate AI into day-to-day operations. Then comes measurement: what actually changed, what still breaks, what needs to be tuned. Once that integration is stable, the engineer moves to the next opportunity. The result is continuous AI progress rather than a one-off project that stalls after launch.

FDEs analyze strategies, discover applications, build agentic frameworks, and roll out AI systems with help from customers' own domain experts and engineers. What they do not do is hand over a strategy deck and disappear.

## Do Forward Deployed Engineers Work with Non-Tech Companies?

Yes—and this is where the question of forward deployed engineer meaning for non-tech companies becomes most relevant. Many non-tech firms have taken shots at deploying AI projects internally without success or quick ROI, with poor vision, lack of talent, skimpy budgets, and underestimating the complexity of deploying AI among the reasons those efforts haven't worked out. The gap between what AI can do and what's actually running in operations is widest precisely in businesses that have never had an in-house engineering team—construction general contractors, trucking and logistics companies, field service operators, distribution businesses, clinic groups, and professional services firms.

Consider what this looks like in practice. A trucking company's dispatch process involves constant judgment calls that an AI-assisted system could handle faster and more consistently—but only if someone maps the existing workflow, connects the right model to the right data, and trains the dispatchers to trust it. A contractor's estimating workflow involves dozens of variables across labor, materials, and subcontractors; AI can accelerate this significantly, but only if the integration fits the tool the estimating team already uses. The use of AI and automation tools is increasing rapidly within logistics companies, and forward deployed engineers make sure that these tools are functioning in actual logistics operations.

Non-tech businesses also face a structural problem: they don't have internal engineers available to do this work. Across the AI industry, startups and enterprise software firms are hiring engineers whose primary task is not building models but helping businesses deploy them inside real operations. For businesses that are not startups or enterprise software firms—the contractor, the clinic, the freight broker—an outside embedded AI operator who owns the AI agenda and implements it is the practical alternative to either hiring a full engineering team or doing nothing.

The term FDE for non-tech companies tends to cause confusion, because it implies a software engineering context that doesn't map onto their world. Embedded AI operator is the same function in plainer language: an outside person with both AI know-how and operations know-how, working inside your business until AI is genuinely running.

## How This Differs from a Consultant, a Freelancer, or a Full-Time Hire

The forward deployed model sits in a gap that the conventional alternatives don't fill well. A large consultancy produces thorough analysis and detailed strategy—but its deliverable is a document. The strategy deck may be excellent; the gap is that nothing gets built, and the team that builds (if any does) is handed a plan written by people who have already left. A forward deployed engineer adds AI execution capability: someone who finds the opportunity, defines the build, ships it, and makes it stick. That is a different job description from a consultant's, and it produces a different output.

A freelancer is narrower still. A freelancer hired to integrate one AI tool into one workflow will do that job and move on. Nobody owns what comes next. The company is left with a single integration, no internal expertise, and no process for finding and acting on the next AI opportunity. Tool sprawl, unconnected systems, and no measurable change are the predictable result.

A full-time head of AI is a large, slow bet. Recruiting takes months. The salary at the senior level required to do this work is significant. And for a startup or a founder-led services business, there may not be enough AI work to fill the role continuously—the need is real, but it arrives in projects, not in a steady full-time stream. As AI models became more capable through 2024–2025, the bottleneck shifted from model performance to deployment execution. That bottleneck is not solved by a hire sitting in a strategy role; it is solved by someone embedded in operations who can build.

The forward deployed model—or its plain-English equivalent, the embedded AI operator—sits between those options. Embedded and ongoing, without the cost or commitment of a senior full-time hire. Narrower and more implementation-focused than a consultancy. Wider in scope and continuity than a freelancer.

## Why Growing Companies Need This Function, Not Just AI Tools

Buying AI tools does not change how a business operates. The tool has to be connected to the right system, configured to fit the actual workflow, and adopted by the people whose jobs it is supposed to improve. In most startups and growing businesses, every person on the team uses AI in their own way—a language model here, an automation there—but nobody owns it at the company level. The result is scattered tool use, duplicated spend, and no measurable difference in how the business actually runs.

The forward deployed engineering role is growing 42-fold because companies realized something crucial: building AI is easy; integrating it into the business is hard. That realization is the whole argument for the function. The tools exist. The gap is in owning the work of getting them into operations—mapping the workflow, choosing the right tool for that workflow, connecting it to existing systems, driving adoption, and then moving to the next opportunity.

As companies move from experimentation to the deployment of AI workflows, new job categories are emerging —and the reason those categories exist is that implementation is a distinct skill from both model development and business strategy. It requires someone who can read a workflow, understand where cost and margin actually sit, and choose and configure AI to fit—not someone who starts with a tool and finds a use case to match it.

- A forward deployed engineer—or embedded AI operator—is not a consultant or a freelancer; they embed inside your business, build working AI integrations, drive adoption, and own the AI agenda continuously.
- The role's value is highest in operations-heavy and non-tech businesses, where the gap between available AI and what's actually running in production is widest and internal engineering capacity is lowest.
- Buying AI tools does not change operations; someone has to map the workflow, configure the tool, connect it to existing systems, and get the team to use it—that is the function an embedded AI operator fills.
- The conventional alternatives—large consultancies, freelancers, and full-time senior AI hires—each leave a gap that the forward deployed model is designed to fill: implementation continuity at a size and cost that fits growing businesses.
